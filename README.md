Sales & Profitability Analysis — Rice & Beans

📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes the sales, expenses, and profitability of two products — Rice and Beans — across January, February, and March.

The analysis was designed to understand business performance, identify profitability trends, compare product performance, and investigate opportunities to reduce unnecessary expenses without negatively affecting sales or product quality.

---

🎯 Business Objective

The main objective was to answer:

«How can expenses be reduced without negatively affecting sales and profitability?»

The analysis focuses on:

- Sales performance
- Expense patterns
- Profitability
- Profit margins
- Expense ratios
- Monthly trends
- Product performance
- Business recommendations

---

🗂️ Dataset

The dataset contains 6 records covering:

- 2 products: Rice and Beans
- 3 months: January, February, March

Original columns

Column| Description
Product| Product being analyzed
Month| Month of the transaction
Sales| Revenue generated
Expenses| Business expenses

Derived columns

Column| Description
Profit| Sales − Expenses
Profit_Margin| Profit as a percentage of Sales
Expense_Ratio| Expenses as a percentage of Sales

📊 Key Findings

Overall Performance

- Total Sales: ₦2,450,000
- Total Expenses: ₦1,430,000
- Total Profit: ₦1,020,000
- Overall Profit Margin: approximately 41.6%

Product Performance

Rice generated the highest total sales and profit over the three-month period.

- Rice Profit: ₦650,000
- Beans Profit: ₦370,000

The average row-level profit margin was approximately:

- Rice: 41.54%
- Beans: 40.95%

Monthly Performance

March recorded the highest:

- Sales: ₦950,000
- Profit: ₦400,000

Profit increased consistently:

January → ₦250,000
February → ₦370,000
March → ₦400,000

February recorded the highest monthly aggregate profit margin at approximately 43.53%.

💡 Business Insights

The analysis suggests that:

- Overall profitability improved throughout the three-month period.
- Rice generated more total profit than Beans.
- March showed the strongest performance in terms of sales and total profit.
- Sales growth should be evaluated alongside profitability rather than sales volume alone.
- Beans had a slightly higher average expense ratio than Rice, although the difference was small.
- Stronger conclusions about cost problems would require more detailed expense data.

---

💼 Recommendations

1. Investigate unnecessary expenses

Management should investigate production, distribution, inventory, wastage, and other operating costs to identify avoidable expenses.

2. Protect expenses that support sales

Costs that contribute to product quality, customer demand, and sales should not be reduced blindly.

3. Investigate Beans-related costs

Because Beans recorded a slightly higher average expense ratio than Rice, management should examine its underlying cost components for possible efficiencies.

4. Monitor profitable sales

Management should continue monitoring products and sales activities that generate higher profits while maintaining appropriate cost controls.

5. Monitor trends before making major decisions

Business decisions should be based on observed sales, expense, and profitability trends rather than sales volume alone.

---

⚠️ Project Limitations

This analysis has some limitations:

- The dataset contains only 6 records.
- The analysis covers only 3 months.
- Expenses are not broken down into specific categories.
- The dataset is suitable for demonstrating analytical techniques but is too small to represent a complete real-world business operation.

Therefore, the findings should be considered directional rather than definitive.

📌 Conclusion

The analysis shows improving profitability across the three-month period, with Rice generating the highest total profit and March recording the strongest monthly sales and profit performance.

The analysis also demonstrates that effective business analysis should consider profitability, expenses, and trends — not sales volume alone.

Further analysis using a larger dataset and more detailed expense categories would provide stronger insights into the specific areas where costs could be optimized.
